# Runbook retrieval evaluation

## What this measures

The investigation agent looks up how to tell failures apart in a small knowledge base: 22 runbooks written like a network operations team's notes (fiber cuts, power failures, bad changes, DNS trouble, and so on, plus general guides and a few topics that never occur in our data). This page measures how well the search over those runbooks finds the right passage when asked a question.

The runbooks are cut into chunks, mostly one per `##` section, and each chunk is stored with a vector (a list of 384 numbers that captures its meaning) and a text index. There are three ways to search:

- **keyword**: classic full-text search. It matches the words of the question against the words of the chunks, and ranks by how well they match. It is exact but blind to synonyms.
- **vector**: the question is turned into a vector with the same local model (`bge-small-en-v1.5`) and the closest chunks win. It handles paraphrases but can miss exact codes such as `LINK_LOS`.
- **hybrid**: both rankings are merged with RRF.

Terms used below:

- **hit rate@k** (also called success@k): the share of queries with at least one correct section in the top k results. This is the easiest number to read: "hit@5 of 0.84" means the answer was in the top 5 for 84% of the questions.
- **recall@k**: of the sections a query should find, the share that appear in the top k results. With three relevant sections, finding one in the top 3 gives 0.33.
- **MRR@10** (mean reciprocal rank): for each query, 1 divided by the rank of the first correct result (1 for first place, 0.5 for second, 0 if not in the top 10), averaged over queries.
- **nDCG@5** (normalised discounted cumulative gain): a score from 0 to 1 that rewards correct results and rewards them more when they are higher in the top 5, compared with a perfect ranking.
- **RRF** (reciprocal rank fusion): to merge rankings, each result scores 1 / (60 + its rank) in every list it appears in, and the scores are added up. Results that rank well in both lists rise to the top.

## How the test was built

There are 69 queries in `evals/retrieval/queries.yaml`, in five types: exact alarm codes (12), paraphrases of what an investigator sees in the data (18), "is it X or Y?" differential questions (15), how-to and reference questions (14), and questions whose best answer is a runbook about a failure that is not in our data (10). Each query lists the sections that answer it (`section_id`, such as `RB-FIBER-CUT/differential-diagnosis`). A query counts as answered when any chunk of such a section is retrieved. A search returns chunks, so the list is reduced to sections first (a second chunk from an already-seen section is skipped).

To keep the test from being too easy, the queries are written from the point of view of what someone sees in the tables ("three sites behind the same link lost all traffic at the same minute"), not from the runbook titles. A unit test fails if a query contains a heading of any runbook, and many queries avoid the runbook's own wording altogether. No query or runbook was edited after seeing these results.

Setup for the numbers: 160 chunks from 153 sections, local Postgres with pgvector, top 20 chunks per query before reduction to sections. The tables are generated by `uv run python -m incident_agent.rag evaluate`. Text outside the markers is written by hand.

## Reading of the results

Numbers below are from the run shown above.

- **In plain terms, hybrid puts a correct section first for about half the queries (hit@1 0.51), in the top 3 for 80% (hit@3 0.80) and in the top 5 for 84% (hit@5 0.84).** The 11 queries it misses entirely at k=5 are listed below. The lower recall figures come mostly from queries with several correct sections, where finding one of them counts only part of the way.
- **Hybrid is the best mode overall and is the default.** It leads on every overall metric (hit@5 0.841, recall@5 0.725, MRR@10 0.652, nDCG@5 0.623). Its advantage over keyword search is clear: the 95% intervals for the hit@5 (+0.10) and MRR (+0.10) differences do not include zero. Its advantage over vector search (hit@5 +0.04, MRR +0.07, recall@5 +0.05) is **not** statistically clear on 69 queries: every interval includes zero. The honest summary is that hybrid is at least as good as vector alone and much better than keyword alone, and that we cannot say more than that with this query set.
- **Each mode has a niche.** On exact alarm codes all three modes find the answer in the top 5 every time (hit@5 1.00), but hybrid ranks it first more often (hit@1 0.83, against 0.75 for vector and 0.50 for keyword). Vector search is level with hybrid on pure paraphrases at k=5 (hit@5 0.78 for both) and ahead of it at k=1 (hit@1 0.44 against 0.33) and on MRR (0.57 against 0.52), a small difference on 18 queries. Keyword search is weakest where there is no shared vocabulary: paraphrases (hit@5 0.61) and distractor topics (0.60).
- **Procedure questions are the hardest** (hybrid hit@5 0.71, recall@5 0.60). Short generic questions such as "a user says traffic dropped, what do I look at first" are pulled towards incident runbooks that talk about traffic drops, away from the triage guide that answers the question.
- **Many misses are near misses.** For example, the question about who owns a failed UPF takeover returns three other sections of the right UPF runbook but not its escalation section. Scored per document instead of per section, the numbers would be higher. We score per section because the agent cites chunks, and a precise chunk is more useful than a related one.
- **Other misses are vocabulary gaps.** "Availability shows ninety-three percent" does not match "93.3" in the text, and "clock reference lost" does not match "GPS" and "timing reference".
- **A BGE query instruction prefix was tried and not adopted.** The model card suggests a prefix for short queries. On this set it moved vector MRR by +0.04 and hybrid MRR by 0.00, which is inside the noise, so the plain query mode of the library is used.
- **Speed.** With the model already loaded, a query takes about 3 ms (keyword), 8 ms (vector) and 12 ms (hybrid), including the embedding of the query. The first ever model load downloaded the files in about 25 s. Later loads take well under a second. Embedding all 160 chunks took 10 s on CPU.

## Limitations

- **The same person wrote the documents and the queries.** This is the main weakness. Queries written by the author of the runbooks tend to share their vocabulary and assumptions, which makes search look better than it would against questions written by someone else, or by the agent's language model. Absolute scores here are probably optimistic. The comparison between the three modes is more trustworthy than the absolute level.
- **The mitigations are partial.** Queries come from observed data patterns, headings are not reused (and a test checks it), several queries are hard paraphrases, and relevance is labelled per section. These reduce the leak but do not remove it. The real test is the agent's own queries in the end-to-end evaluation (M5).
- **The knowledge base is small.** 153 sections in total, so a top-5 list covers 3% of the corpus. A larger knowledge base would make every score lower.
- **The query set is small.** With 69 queries, differences below about 0.1 are within noise (see the interval table). Per-type rows have 10 to 18 queries each and are only indicative.
- **Labels are judgements.** Several sections answer the same question (for example the alarm reference and a runbook's signature section). We listed all the sections we judged relevant, but a reasonable reader might accept others that the test counts as misses. For queries with several relevant sections, recall@1 cannot reach 1.0. The best possible value is shown under the overall table.
- **One embedding model, one chunking.** Results say nothing about other models or chunk sizes. The model is the quantised ONNX build of `bge-small-en-v1.5` that fastembed downloads.
- **The keyword settings were fixed in advance.** Terms are joined with OR, ranked with `ts_rank_cd` and length normalisation, using the English dictionary. They were not tuned on these queries.
